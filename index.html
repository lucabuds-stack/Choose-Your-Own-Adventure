Choose Your Own Adventure

England, late 16th century. After a long day travelling between villages, you and your horse are exhausted. As night falls, you stop at a tavern for a hot meal and a place to rest. While waiting for your supper, the warmth of the fire and the murmur of conversation make your eyelids heavy. Before long, you fall asleep at your table. CRACK! A sharp pain shoots through your forehead. You wake with a start as a metal spoon clatters onto the table in front of you. Laughter echoes around the tavern. Someone has thrown a spoon at your head. As you rub the sore spot on your forehead, you notice a stranger across the room grinning at you. What do you do?

You decide to confront the stranger. In a tavern like this, an insult cannot go unanswered. If you let it slide, you risk losing the respect of the crowd, and with a rough bunch like this, a lack of respect can lead to far greater trouble before the night is over. You rise from your chair. The tavern grows quieter as nearby patrons notice your angry stance. You look at the stranger and start walking towards him…

With your forehead still throbbing, you make your way towards the stranger, keeping your eyes fixed on him.

"This is not quite the welcome I was expecting in your village," you say mildly.

The man rises from his chair, still looking hostile, but confused by your reaction. But the moment he gets a good look at you, his expression changes completely. The hostility drains completely from his face, replaced by embarrassment.

"My apologies, stranger," he says. "My eyesight is not what it once was. I mistook you for Goldfinger Gregor, the village goldsmith." The tavern erupts into laughter; the man blushes and lowers his head. "You see, Gregor and I have an ongoing disagreement. He refuses to return a precious bow and quiver of arrows that belonged to my late father." He sighs and shakes his head. "When I saw you asleep by the fire, I thought I'd finally have my revenge."

He pauses and looks at you apologetically. "Clearly, I chose the wrong man. The next round is on me, sit down and I will tell you more about my troubles with Gregor, my name is William."

The stranger gestures to the empty seat opposite him, while a fresh pint is placed. "My wife says I should stop quarrelling with the goldsmith and instead search for the evidence proving the bow belongs to my family," he says. "But every time I see Gregor's smug face, I feel more inclined to knock out his teeth than search through old papers."

The tavern patrons chuckle knowingly. "Tell me, traveller," he says, leaning forward. "What would you do in my position?"

You have heard one too many stories of greedy goldsmiths who, after years have passed, conveniently "forget" who owns the precious items left in their safes.

More than one hardworking family has been deprived of its inheritance by a craftsman hiding behind missing records and convenient excuses.

Besides, William strikes you as an honest man. He seems genuinely distressed by the loss of his father's heirloom, and you have little reason to doubt his word.

You glance at William. Despite his determination, he is not an imposing figure.

"I will come with you," you declare. "I would be honoured to help you confront this greedy bastard and settle the matter." William's face lights up.

"Thank you, friend. Let us be there tomorrow, first thing in the morning. As a token of my gratitude, I promise three of the golden arrows, if we can get the quiver back. They are each worth a small fortune in themselves; but first let me buy you some more pints."

The next morning, the two of you make your way to Gregory Goldfinger's workshop. The sound of hammering echoes into the street long before you reach the door. Inside, Gregory is bent over an anvil, shaping a glowing piece of iron. When he notices William, he lets out an exasperated groan. "Oh, for heaven's sake, William. You again?". He sets down his hammer.

"I have already told you a dozen times. I cannot hand over the quiver based solely on your word. Only an idiot would entrust such a valuable heirloom to a man who cannot produce a shred of evidence proving it belongs to him." Gregory's eyes move towards you. He snorts. "And who might this be? Your bodyguard? Get out of my workshop, you and your moron-looking friend."

Your reaction is immediate. Before reason has a chance to intervene, your fist shoots forward.

CRACK!

The punch lands squarely on Gregory's nose. The goldsmith staggers backwards, clutching his face in shock, blood begins to drip from his face.

For a moment, the workshop falls completely silent. Then, from outside, a voice shouts: "Fight! Fight at the goldsmith's!".

Within moments, the workshop descends into chaos.

Villagers pour through the doorway to protect Gregory and his workshop, which not only serves as his place of business but also safeguards the savings and valuables of many families in Greenward. Voices rise, insults, accusations, and threats fly through the air, shoves are exchanged, a stool topples over, someone breaks a lantern....the situation is rapidly spiralling out of control.

You brace yourself for the fight of your life. Then, suddenly, a commanding voice cuts through the uproar.

"WHAT IS ALL THIS COMMOTION?"

The room falls silent. The village constable has arrived.

Tall, broad-shouldered, and stern-faced, he steps into the workshop with an authority that immediately commands respect. Gregory is the first to speak: red-faced and still clutching his injured nose, he angrily recounts the events that led to the disturbance. William attempts to interrupt, but the constable silences both men with a raised hand.

"Enough." The single word echoes through the workshop.

"This quarrel has disturbed the peace of my village for far too long." He turns to William.

"You cannot claim ownership of such a valuable artefact without evidence. No document supports your claim, and nobody in Greenward remembers your family possessing the quiver." Then he faces Gregory.

"But neither can you truly claim it as your own. The quiver has remained in your possession for over fifty years without a proven owner."

The constable pauses before delivering his judgement. "Therefore, I order that neither of you shall possess it." "The quiver shall become the property of the village of Greenward."

"It will be placed in the church, displayed within a glass cabinet, where it shall remain safe and where every resident and traveller may admire it."

For the first time that day, neither William nor Gregory has a reply.

The constable's decision is final.

A few days later, the ancient quiver is proudly displayed in the village church. Before long, travellers begin arriving from distant towns to admire the remarkable artefact and hear the story of the dispute that nearly tore Greenward apart.

The village prospers from the attention.

As for you, you soon complete the final stretch of your journey and return home. Though William never recovered his father's heirloom, and Gregory never retained possession of it, the quarrel finally came to an end.

And for years afterwards, you are remembered as the traveller whose intervention ultimately brought peace, and a bit of fame, to the small village of Greenward.

The old hermit returns peacefully to his beloved cave. Gregory earns the respect of the village for doing what is right. And you continue the final stage of your journey home, satisfied that, on this occasion, truth and patience proved stronger than anger and suspicion.

As if to make matters worse, a gust of wind slams the lid of the chest shut with a loud BANG! For a brief moment, you could swear you hear faint ghostly laughter echoing across the cliffs.

Defeated, you climb back onto your horse and begin the long journey home. From that day onwards, whenever someone tells you that honesty is the best policy, you simply sigh...and quietly agree.

With your forehead still throbbing, you approach him. Striding across the tavern, you seize the stranger by the front of his tunic and slam him against the wall. He is smaller than you expected, in a fair fight, you are confident you could easily defeat him, but a dozen men leap to their feet. Chairs scrape across the floor. The stranger says: "I don't like strangers," he says. "Especially fiery ones." The tavern falls silent. He continues: "In this village, we do not settle quarrels with fists. Every man is given a chance to prove his worth at the butts. At sunrise, you and I shall shoot three arrows each. The better archer wins the dispute."

At sunrise, you arrive promptly at the butts. A crowd has already gathered, eager to witness the contest. Men, women, and children line the field, murmuring excitedly as they watch the preparations.

You feel confident in your archery skills and, truthfully, you admire the civil way this strange village settles its disputes. Still, before agreeing to compete, you want to know exactly what is at stake.

Standing in the centre of the field is the Challenge Master, an elderly man holding two longbows and a bundle of ten arrows. "It is wise of you to ask," he says. He gestures toward the villagers.

"You should first know that Greenwalt is famous for its archers. The secrets of the bow have been passed down through generations."

The Duel Master pauses before continuing. "An archery contest is how we settle disputes. If you win, you will be rewarded for your bravery and skill. Your honour will be recognised, and the village will owe you a debt." The crowd nods approvingly.

"But if you lose," he says gravely, "the people of Greenwalt shall decide your fate. For one full day, you must serve the village and obey the tasks assigned to you."

A ripple of laughter spreads through the crowd. Some villagers grin mischievously. Others exchange knowing glances. The stranger who challenged you smiles. "Do not worry, traveller," he says. "Most survive the experience."

The crowd erupts in laughter once more.

The Duel Master raises a hand for silence.

"Now you understand what is at stake," he declares. He points towards two chalk circles marked on the ground.

"Take your place in the circles. The contest is about to begin."

You take your place in the circle and nock your first arrow. The crowd falls silent. Drawing the bowstring back, you take careful aim and release. The arrow flies straight and true, striking the centre of the target.

A murmur of surprise spreads through the villagers. Your opponent seems momentarily unsettled. He had clearly not expected an outsider to possess such skill with a bow.

Then he takes his turn.

His arrow cuts through the morning air and lands perfectly in the centre of the target.

The crowd erupts in applause.

Your confidence wavers, but you steady your breathing and focus on the next shot.

Again, your arrow finds the centre. And again, your opponent matches it perfectly.

The contest continues in this fashion, arrow after arrow. Each shot is met with gasps and cheers as the two of you demonstrate remarkable accuracy.

Soon, only one arrow remains. Your opponent steps forward to take the final shot. If he succeeds, he will win the challenge. If he fails, victory will be yours.

The village watches in complete silence. The stranger raises his bow and takes aim.

Then, at the worst possible moment, a sudden gust of wind sweeps dust into his face. His nose twitches. He tries to resist, but fails. A mighty sneeze bursts from him just as he releases the string. The arrow flies wildly past the target. For a moment, nobody speaks. You can hardly believe your luck, you have won.

The crowd's reaction is far from cheerful. Whistles and groans echo across the field, and several villagers call for the contest to be repeated. But the Duel Master remains unmoved. "A shot released is a shot judged," he declares. "The contest is over. The traveller has won. May satisfaction and pride be his reward"

The villagers reluctantly and quietly accept the verdict. You expected to be cheered, or a heftier reward, but truthfully, you are simply relieved. Your journey has been long and dangerous. You have faced storms, thieves, and countless hardships upon the road.

Now, home is only a day's ride away.

As you mount your horse and leave Greenwalt behind, you cannot help but smile. Your family awaits you, eager to hear tales of your adventures. And among them all, none will be more remarkable than the story of the traveller who became the first outsider ever to defeat a villager of Greenwalt in an archery contest.

"Oh, and one more thing," says the Duel Master with a grin. "To keep your spirits high, the villagers are permitted to encourage you by throwing eggs and rotten vegetables." A basket of rotten cabbages is immediately distributed among the crowd. You stare in disbelief. Perhaps accepting the challenge had not been your wisest decision.

The hours that follow are the longest and most exhausting of your life. You haul stones. You spread mortar. You hammer. You sweat. Every few minutes, an egg explodes against your back. A rotten cabbage narrowly misses your head.

Some mischievous child discovers that overripe tomatoes are remarkably accurate projectiles.

The laughter of the villagers echoes through the valley from dawn until dusk. By sunset, however, something extraordinary has happened. The once muddy, uneven road through Greenwalt has become the finest road for miles around.

The Mayor proudly inspects your work. "Well done, stranger," he declares. "You may have lost the contest, but you have done our village a great service."

He shakes your aching hand. "From this day forward, you shall always be welcome in Greenwalt as an honoured guest." You force a tired smile. Honour is all very well. But as you limp away from the village with egg dripping from your hat and cabbage leaves clinging to your cloak, you make yourself one solemn promise: You will never, ever challenge a Greenwalt villager to an archery contest again.


The End

After your meal, you retire to your room, but sleep refuses to come.

Your mind is troubled by the challenge awaiting you at dawn.

You know that refusing the contest would be seen as cowardly, especially after confronting the stranger so boldly before the entire tavern. Yet your journey is almost at an end. Along the way, you have survived storms, pirates, highwaymen, and countless dangers. The thought of risking your life over a spoon thrown by a fool seems increasingly absurd. Besides, the people of Greenwalt are renowned for their archery. The bow is their pride and heritage, while you have always been far more skilled with a sword than an arrow. The longer you think about it, the more obvious the answer becomes.

Under the cover of darkness, you quietly gather your belongings and make your way downstairs. The tavern is silent. Outside, your horse rests peacefully beneath a shelter. Nearby, the stable boy snores softly in a wooden chair.

Careful not to make a sound, you begin saddling your horse.

Then disaster strikes. As you untie the reins, your elbow accidentally bumps the horse in the neighbouring stall.

The startled animal lets out a loud, piercing neigh. The stable boy jerks awake. For a moment, he stares at you in confusion. Then his eyes widen. "The challenged foreigner is running away!" he shouts at the top of his lungs.

Lights immediately flare to life across the village.

Doors burst open.

And from somewhere in the darkness, you hear the unmistakable sound of dozens of footsteps running towards the stables.

Before you can even think of escaping, villagers appear from every direction, astonishingly alert for the middle of the night. You rush towards your horse, hoping to mount it before anyone can stop you.

Too late.

You spend the remainder of the night in a damp, musty prison cell beneath the village hall. The following morning, you are brought before a makeshift tribunal presided over by the sheriƯ himself. He listens to the accusations before solemnly announcing his judgement. "You accepted the challenge before the people of Greenwalt," he declares. "Then you attempted to flee like a coward. By mocking our sacred customs, you have dishonoured both yourself and this village." He stares at you severely: "I sentence you to one month in the village prison."

The sentence proves far worse than you imagined. Each day is almost identical to the last. Breakfast consists of stale bread. Lunch consists of stale bread. Supper... is slightly older stale bread.

The rats soon become your closest companions. After a week, you begin giving them names. After two weeks, you find yourself looking forward to the daily visit of a single fly that buzzes lazily around your cell. By the end of the month, you are almost sad to see it leave through a crack in the wall.

The experience gives you plenty of time to reflect. Perhaps you should never have accepted the challenge. Or perhaps you should simply have faced it with dignity. Either way, one lesson is certain: the people of Greenwalt are the strangest folk you have ever encountered. When your sentence finally ends, you mount your horse without looking back and make yourself one solemn promise: no matter how tired you are, you will never again spend the night in Greenwalt.

You slow your horse and finally allow yourself a smile. You have escaped.

The remainder of your journey passes without incident, and a few days later you arrive safely home, where your family greets you with warm embraces and a hearty meal.

When they ask about your adventures, you tell them many stories, but somehow leave out the part about fleeing an archery challenge in the middle of the night….some tales are better left untold.

Years later, whenever your trading routes take you anywhere near Greenwalt, you make a considerable detour.

Not because you fear the villagers...but because you have no wish to discover whether they are still waiting for the foreign merchant who never kept his appointment at the butts.
